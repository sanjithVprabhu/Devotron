# 01 — Glossary

> Definitive terminology used throughout the spec and the codebase. **When in doubt, refer here.** Inconsistent terminology is the #1 source of architectural confusion.

---

## Core Concepts

### Dev
The single, multi-channel meta-agent that serves as the entry point to the platform. Dev exists as one logical agent across WhatsApp, Twitter, and (V2) Telegram/Instagram. Dev does **not** operate any specific business — it onboards business owners, routes consumers to relevant businesses, and remembers users across sessions. Dev has its own persona that adapts to the user but stays internally consistent.

Dev is **not** a per-tenant agent. There is exactly one Dev. Multiple end-users talk to Dev concurrently.

### Business
A real-world organization (or aspiring organization) that has been onboarded onto the platform. Each Business has exactly one **Blueprint** and one or more **Business Agents** running its operations. A Business has an **Owner** (a Principal) and zero or more **Team Members** (Principals).

### Blueprint (Business Blueprint)
The central versioned artifact for each Business. A structured JSON document that captures everything about the business: identity, vertical, persona, catalog, capabilities, policies, integrations, team, daemon configuration. **All runtime behavior of the Business Agent derives from the Blueprint.** Blueprint mutations are events.

See `06_BUSINESS_BLUEPRINT.md` for the complete schema.

### Business Agent
The per-tenant runtime that operates a single Business. Reads from that business's current Blueprint version. Composed of multiple sub-agents under a Supervisor. Stateless across requests; state lives in events + databases.

### Capability Worker (or just "Capability")
A stateless function or service that does one specific thing — `catalog.search`, `payment.razorpay.create_link`, `broadcast.send`, `scheduling.calendar.book`. Business Agents invoke capabilities as tool calls. Verticals are bundles of capabilities.

### Principal
A unique identity in the system. A Principal is the abstract identity that can hold one or more Roles. WhatsApp phone numbers, Twitter handles, and email addresses are **Identifiers** that resolve to a Principal. One human person can have one Principal across multiple identifiers (cross-channel identity).

A Principal can hold any of these Roles:
- **EndUser** — interacts with Dev or with Business Agents as a customer/lead
- **BusinessOwner** — created and owns a Business; root admin
- **BusinessTeamMember** — invited to a Business with specific permissions
- **BusinessAgent** — yes, agents are Principals too. Required for the Agent-to-Agent protocol (V2).

See `07_IDENTITY_AND_PRINCIPALS.md` for full identity model.

### Vertical
A category of business that shares structural characteristics. Verticals determine which capability bundle is loaded by default and which intake question tree Dev uses during onboarding. v1 verticals: **Auto Parts (B2C/B2B Retail)**, **Jobs (Aggregator)**. Future verticals will be added as we expand.

A Business has exactly one vertical (or `"generic"` as fallback).

### Daemon
The proactive intelligence layer. A scheduled, sandboxed process per tenant that reads the conversation event stream, identifies opportunities (re-engagement candidates, FAQ patterns, catalog gaps, problematic conversations), and generates **Daemon Proposals** for owner approval.

The daemon is **not** in the request path. It runs on a schedule, bounded by per-tenant compute budget.

### Daemon Proposal
A structured suggestion generated by the Daemon, queued for the Business Owner to review in the dashboard. Examples: "Send re-engagement message to 47 customers who haven't ordered in 60+ days," "Add 'COD available' to FAQ — asked 12 times this week."

### Event (in the event-sourcing sense)
An immutable record of a meaningful state transition or action, written to Kafka and persisted. Examples: `MessageReceived`, `AgentResponded`, `ToolCalled`, `BlueprintMutated`, `OrderPlaced`, `EscalationTriggered`, `DaemonProposalCreated`, `OwnerApproved`, `A2ATransactionInitiated`.

Events are the source of truth for analytics, replay, and the daemon's input stream.

### Conversation
A logical thread of messages between one EndUser and one Agent (Dev or a Business Agent), on one channel. Has a start, may have escalations, may end. Conversations are addressable for replay and review.

### Session
The shorter-lived state for an active conversation. WhatsApp's 24-hour customer service window is a session boundary. After 24 hours of EndUser inactivity, sending a message requires a Meta-approved template.

### Channel
A messaging platform we operate on. v1: WhatsApp (full), Twitter (Dev-only). v1.5/V2: Telegram, Instagram.

A Channel Adapter is the code layer that translates between the channel's API and our internal canonical message format.

## Agent Architecture Terms

### Supervisor
The top-level routing agent within a Business Agent. Receives an incoming message, decides which sub-agent should handle it, dispatches, and integrates the result.

### Sub-Agent
A specialized agent within a Business Agent. Examples: Catalog Agent, Transaction Agent, Support Agent, Follow-up Agent, Escalation Agent. Each is a focused LLM call (often with its own prompt and tools) coordinated by the Supervisor.

### Tool Call
An invocation of a Capability by an agent. Tool calls are recorded as events for audit and replay.

### Harness
The infrastructure around an LLM call: prompt assembly, context injection, tool definitions, retry logic, cost tracking, response parsing, evaluation hooks. VEDA uses a custom harness optimized for cost control and prompt iteration.

### Persona
The configured personality of an agent. For Dev, persona is one global config that adapts per user. For Business Agents, persona is layered: vertical default + owner config + per-customer adaptive.

### Vertical Bundle
A pre-configured set of Capabilities, default Persona traits, intake question tree, and runtime workflow templates that ship with a Vertical.

### Intake Question Tree
The opinionated, vertical-specific sequence of questions Dev asks during business onboarding to fill out the Blueprint. Not a flowchart — a question graph with conditional branches.

## Identity & Tenancy

### Tenant
An isolated unit of multi-tenancy. In v1, **one Business = one Tenant.** Postgres rows, Mongo databases, Qdrant collections, and Kafka topics are scoped by tenant ID.

### Tenant ID
Stable opaque identifier (UUID) for a Tenant. Used as the primary key for isolation enforcement.

### Role
A named set of permissions a Principal holds within a Business. v1 roles: Owner, Admin, Operator, Viewer (custom roles V2).

### Permission
A specific action gated by a Role. Examples: `blueprint.mutate`, `conversation.takeover`, `team.invite`, `billing.view`.

### Cross-Channel Identity (CCI)
The mechanism by which Dev recognizes a user who first interacted on one channel (e.g., Twitter) when they reach out on another (e.g., WhatsApp). Implemented via short-lived linking codes. See `07_IDENTITY_AND_PRINCIPALS.md`.

## Operational Terms

### Blueprint Mutation
Any change to a Business Blueprint. Generates a new Blueprint version. Recorded as a `BlueprintMutated` event with the diff and the Principal who triggered it.

### Owner Approval Flow
A structured human-in-the-loop pattern. Used for: bounded haggling, daemon proposals, A2A external transactions, team permission grants. **Not** a fallback for "agent unsure" — those go to Escalation.

### Escalation
A handoff from agent to a human team member (or the Owner). Triggered by explicit rules in the Blueprint (e.g., "queries about returns always escalate") or by agent-detected confusion (low confidence on a critical decision).

### Shared Inbox
The dashboard surface where Team Members can take over live conversations from the agent. Multi-agent assignment, internal notes, conversation history.

### 24-Hour Window
WhatsApp's customer service window — a Business can send free-form messages to a customer for 24 hours after the customer's last inbound message. After that window, only Meta-approved templates can be sent. Must be tracked per Conversation in Redis.

### Template (Message Template)
A pre-approved message format that Meta has reviewed for outbound messaging outside the 24-hour window. Templates have variables. Each template requires Meta approval (1–7 days). The platform manages template lifecycle.

### Quality Rating
Meta's per-phone-number quality score (Green/Yellow/Red). Affected by user blocks, complaints, and engagement. Red rating restricts messaging. The platform monitors and reacts to quality changes.

## Money & Commerce

### Order
A logical unit of commerce: customer commits to buy specific items (or services or a job application) at a specific price. Orders move through states: Created → Confirmed → Paid → Fulfilled → Closed (or cancelled at any stage).

### Catalog Item
A unit in a business's catalog. Schema varies by vertical (auto parts have SKUs, jobs have JD/CTC, etc.) — stored in Mongo because of heterogeneity.

### A2A Transaction
A Business-to-Business transaction conducted between two Business Agents using the structured Agent-to-Agent Protocol. V2 feature; v1 has hooks but no live execution.

### Reputation Score (V2)
A computed score for a Business, derived from completed transaction history, user reviews (collected silently in v1), and platform-internal signals.

## Costs & Limits

### Per-Tenant Compute Budget
A daily LLM-cost cap per tenant. The Daemon and runtime obey this cap. Exceeding cap throttles non-essential operations (Daemon first, then non-customer-facing). See `20_OBSERVABILITY_AND_COST.md`.

### Per-Conversation Cost
The aggregated LLM + infrastructure cost of a single conversation. Tracked and reported in the dashboard. Target: under ₹1.50 average for SMB tier.

### Usage Tier
The pricing plan a Tenant is on (Free, Starter, Growth, Pro, Enterprise). Determines feature access, capability bundle limits, daemon budget, included template count.

## Things That Are *Not* Defined Here But People Will Ask

- **"Workflow"** — we deliberately avoid this term because it implies flow-builder competitors. We use **Intake Question Tree** (for onboarding) or **Capability Sequence** (for runtime).
- **"Bot"** — we use **Agent**. Bots are dumb; agents reason.
- **"Customer"** vs **"Lead"** vs **"User"** — we use **EndUser** for the Principal Role. The Business may internally distinguish between leads, prospects, customers — that's their CRM concern, not ours.
- **"Skill"** — we use **Capability**. Skill is overloaded (it's also a Claude/LLM term).
